# **Empathy as the Last Mile: Why AI Doesn't Win When It Ignores the Gut Feeling**

*By Dr. Chukwuma Onyeije | Maternal–Fetal Medicine*

---

## **Introduction: The Part of Care Patients Never Forget**

When patients walk into an exam room, they bring more than symptoms.
They bring fear, past experiences, family stories, late-night Google searches, and unspoken questions they're afraid to ask.

AI can recognize patterns in data.
But it cannot recognize a trembling hand.
It cannot hear the hesitation in a patient's voice when they say "I'm okay."
It cannot look someone in the eyes and see that they are not.

And that is why empathy — the real, human kind — is the last mile of clinical care.

AI can help us reach the destination faster,
but only a human being can walk the last steps with the patient.

---

## **What Patients Are Telling Us (Even When They Don't Say It)**

Patients rarely say "I'm scared."
They say:

* "So… is this normal?"
* "If it were your mother, what would you do?"
* "I just want to be okay."
* "I Googled this and now I'm confused."

These are not medical questions.
They are emotional check-ins.
They are requests for reassurance, presence, and partnership.

AI can answer a medical question.
But only a clinician can answer a human one.

---

## **Where AI Helps — and Where It Stops**

AI has changed the first half of care dramatically:

* It can summarize a visit
* Suggest a differential
* Draft a note
* Pull out billing codes
* Help explain lab results
* Provide education
* Offer 24/7 access

And patients appreciate this.
They use it night and day because it feels safe, accessible, and nonjudgmental.

But then they hit a wall.

AI cannot:

* Share the room's silence
* Catch the quiver in a worried parent's voice
* Sit with someone who is grieving
* Hold the tension of uncertainty
* Guide a family through a dangerous pregnancy
* Hear the history behind the question

And it cannot deliver the words that only come from lived human experience —
"I know this is scary. Let's walk through it together."

---

## **Empathy Isn't a Side Skill — It's the Foundation of Healing**

We sometimes talk about empathy like a bonus skill, a soft skill, something nice to have.

But patients see it differently.

Empathy is:

* Trust
* Safety
* Understanding
* Respect
* Partnership
* Guidance

When patients say they "like" a doctor, what they mean is:
"I feel understood."

That feeling cannot be automated.
It can be supported, extended, amplified — but not replaced.

---

## **Why Patients Are Turning to AI First**

Here is the truth many physicians struggle to hear:

Patients are turning to AI not because they distrust doctors,
but because they cannot reach us when they need us most.

AI answers:

* Immediately
* Without rushing
* Without judgment
* Without making them feel like a burden

Patients aren't choosing AI over humans.
They are choosing it over silence, delays, and broken systems.

This is not a threat.
It is a message.

If AI is meeting patients in the first mile,
physicians must own the last mile.

---

## **The Last Mile: What Only Humans Can Deliver**

### **1. Reassurance in Uncertainty**

There is nothing scarier than "we're not sure yet."
AI can explain possibilities.
But only a human can make uncertainty bearable.

### **2. Meaningful touch**

Resting a hand on a shoulder.
Pausing before giving difficult news.
These moments change everything.

### **3. Shared humanity**

A patient needs to know you've cared for others like them.
You've seen this.
You've walked with families through this.
You're not guessing — you're guiding.

### **4. Moral judgment**

AI can tell you what's statistically possible.
Only a clinician can tell you what's responsible.

### **5. Hope**

Not false hope — grounded hope.
The kind that says:
"We don't know everything, but we won't face it alone."

Hope cannot be automated.

---

## **How AI Can Strengthen Empathy Instead of Replacing It**

Empathy is not threatened by AI.
It is strengthened by it — if we use the tools correctly.

AI can free clinicians from:

* Note writing
* Coding
* Billing documentation
* Prior auth language
* Hours of chart reviews
* Click-based EMR fatigue
* "Pajama time" documentation

When those tasks disappear, clinicians regain the one resource empathy requires:

**Time.**

Time to listen.
Time to reflect.
Time to slow down.
Time to be human again.

In other words:

AI doesn't replace empathy.
It protects it.

---

## **Patients Don't Want Perfect Doctors — They Want Present Ones**

The myth that patients want superhuman accuracy is false.

They want:

* Someone who hears them
* Someone who sees them
* Someone who advocates for them
* Someone who speaks with compassion
* Someone who stands in their fear with them

AI cannot be present.
It cannot share the emotional weight of illness.

It can deliver information.
But only humans can deliver care.

---

## **Conclusion: The Heart of Medicine Still Beats**

The future of medicine isn't AI versus clinicians.
It is AI beside clinicians — supporting, accelerating, and amplifying what humans alone can do.

AI is the new first mile.
Empathy is the last mile.

Patients need both.
But only one of them remembers your face, your voice, your kindness, and your commitment long after the visit ends.

In the end, AI may help save time —
but empathy saves people.
