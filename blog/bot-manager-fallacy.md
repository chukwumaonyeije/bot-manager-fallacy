# 🧠 **The Bot Manager Fallacy: Why Doctors Won't Be Supervising AI — And What We Should Be Training For Instead**

*By Dr. Chukwuma Onyeije, Maternal–Fetal Medicine | Founder, Doctors Who Code*  

---

> [!NOTE]
> **TL;DR:** The belief that physicians will "supervise AI forever" is economically unsustainable.  
> AI will replace cognitive tasks — not physicians — but only if we embrace technical literacy, procedural skill, and the deeply human elements of medicine.  
> The future belongs to physicians who *lead* AI workflows, not those who fear them.

---

# **Introduction: Abstraction Has Always Changed Medicine**

Every major shift in medicine has been driven by **abstraction** — tools that remove layers of human judgment and replace them with structured, more reliable processes.

- Stethoscope → **Echocardiogram**  
- Chest X-ray hunch → **CT-confirmed pulmonary embolism**  
- Textbooks → **Google**  
- Google → **ChatGPT + clinical reasoning models**  

Each abstraction took work physicians once believed was uniquely human…  
and handed it to a tool that could do it:

- faster  
- cheaper  
- more reliably  

**Artificial intelligence is the next abstraction layer — and the most disruptive one we've ever faced.**

Yet many clinicians still believe:

> "AI will help with the routine work. I will still be the one in charge."

It feels comforting.  
It feels safe.  
But it is economically flawed.

This belief has a name.

---

# ⚠️ **The Bot Manager Fallacy**

The **Bot Manager Fallacy** is the belief that physicians will sit at the top of an AI pyramid:

- reviewing outputs  
- approving diagnoses  
- billing for oversight  
- while the AI does the actual cognitive heavy lifting  

It assumes hospitals will pay doctors $250/hour to *click "Approve."*

> [!WARNING]
> **But if an AI system can diagnose, counsel, document, and recommend treatment accurately…  
> why would any health system pay a physician to rubber-stamp what is already done?**

This is not a regulatory question.  
This is an **economic** one.

And in medicine, **economics always wins.**

---

# 💸 **The Economics Are Not on Our Side**

AI is:

- Fast  
- Scalable  
- Never fatigued  
- Always available  
- Extremely cheap  

Physicians are:

- Slow (because we must be careful)  
- Limited in number  
- Expensive  
- Fatigable  

Once AI reliability stabilizes, the **human approver becomes the most expensive node in the workflow.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Across industries, "human in the loop" phases are:
> 1. **Necessary at first**  
> 2. **Reduced over time**  
> 3. **Eventually automated**  
>
> Medicine will not be different.

---

# 🌍 **The First Domino Isn't Radiology — It's Primary Care**

Everyone assumes radiology will fall first.

But the real first domino is **primary care**, because of:

- national physician shortages  
- expanding medical deserts  
- retail clinics  
- virtual care scaling  
- political pressure for access  

AI clinics will appear **first** in communities where patients currently have *no doctor at all*.

Once it proves safe and affordable there, it expands everywhere.

This isn't speculation — **it's policy logic.**

---

# 🤖 **Patients Already Trust AI More Than We Think**

Recent studies show:

> **AI-generated patient messages were rated *more empathetic* than doctor responses.**

Why?

Physicians are constrained by:

- 15-minute visits  
- burnout  
- EMR overload  
- time scarcity  

AI, in contrast:

- has infinite time  
- writes full paragraphs  
- answers thoroughly  
- never interrupts  

If a patient wants **time, patience, clarity**, AI may become their *preferred* first stop.

This should not scare us.  
It should **inform** us.

---

# ⚖️ **Liability Will Not Protect Physician Jobs**

Doctors ask:

> "Who gets sued when AI makes a mistake?"

The answer:

**Whoever profits from the AI.**

Companies can:

- buy malpractice coverage  
- indemnify health systems  
- distribute risk  
- create new legal structures  

Liability will *slow* adoption.  
But it will **not stop** it.

There is too much momentum — economic, political, and technological.

---

# 🔥 **What AI Will Replace**

AI will **not** replace medicine.  
But it **will** replace specific cognitive tasks:

- First-pass triage  
- Guideline-based reasoning  
- Common-condition diagnosis  
- Portal messages  
- Follow-up counseling  
- Documentation  
- Medical coding  
- Prior authorization workflows  

> [!CAUTION]
> **AI will erode the physician's cognitive monopoly.**  
> This is the part we must confront honestly.

---

# 🩺 **What AI *Cannot* Replace — The Physician Moat**

Here is the good news:

AI can outperform humans on pattern recognition, guidelines, and data synthesis.  
But it fails — profoundly — on the most **human** aspects of care.

---

## **1. High-Nuance History Taking**

Extracting the real story requires:

- cultural intelligence  
- emotional sensitivity  
- reading shame and fear  
- understanding subtext  
- eliciting truth  

> [!NOTE]
> **AI is only as good as its inputs.  
> Humans remain the stewards of input quality.**

This is a durable moat.

---

## **2. Procedural Competence**

Hands-on tasks remain the hardest to automate:

- deliveries  
- ultrasound-guided procedures  
- placental interventions  
- surgical care  
- real-time maternal–fetal management  

This moat protects physicians who maintain procedural skill.

---

## **3. Ethical Reasoning and Human Leadership**

AI cannot manage:

- end-of-life decisions  
- family conflict  
- cultural nuance  
- religion  
- trust-building  
- deep empathy  

Medicine is not just logic.  
It is **relationship**.

---

## **4. Technical Literacy (Doctors Who Code)**

The future physician must understand:

- how LLMs reason  
- how they hallucinate  
- how to structure workflows  
- when not to trust the machine  
- how to build AI-augmented systems  

> [!TIP]
> This is why **Doctors Who Code** and **CodeCraftMD** exist —  
> to train physicians to **lead** AI workflows, not just use them.

---

# 🚀 **How Physicians Can Thrive in the AI Era**

Here is the path forward.

---

## **1. Become the Best Historians in the Healthcare System**

AI cannot fix a bad history.  
But a clinician can improve the input that AI relies on.

---

## **2. Increase Procedural Capability**

The more your work involves **hands-on** skill, the safer your career becomes.

---

## **3. Understand How AI Models Think**

Not as engineers — but as clinicians who can detect failure modes.

---

## **4. Build AI-Augmented Clinical Workflows**

This is the future:

**Ambient Scribe → EvidenceMD → CodeCraftMD → EMR**

A closed loop that:

- reduces burnout  
- improves documentation  
- strengthens clinical reasoning  

---

## **5. Train Students and Residents Differently**

They must learn:

- how to think *with* AI  
- how to question AI  
- how to avoid overreliance  
- how to maintain human judgment  

This **must** become the new medical literacy.

---

# 🧩 **Conclusion: AI Won't Replace Doctors — But It Will Replace What Doctors Do**

The **Bot Manager Fallacy** — the belief we will supervise AI indefinitely — is comforting but false.

The truth is:

- AI will take work that burns us out  
- AI will erode routine cognitive tasks  
- AI will elevate patient communication  
- AI will force us to return to what is *deeply human*  

The question is not:

> "Will AI replace doctors?"

The real question is:

> **Will we lead this transition — or be automated by it?**

At **Doctors Who Code**, I believe the future physician will be:

- AI-augmented  
- Technically literate  
- Procedurally skilled  
- Deeply human  

If we embrace that identity, we won't merely survive the AI transition —  
**we will shape it.**
